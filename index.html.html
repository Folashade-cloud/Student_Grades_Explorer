Exploring the factors associated with passing the final grade

Folashade Ogunajo and Vanessa Ogoru

Information Visualisation, 2026/2027

Department of Electronics, Telecommunications and Informatics (DETI), University of Aveiro

This dashboard explores how the study habits, family background and social life of secondary-school students in Portugal relate to their final grades. Grades go from 0 to 20, and students need at least 10 to pass.

Questions you can answer here

  1. Which factors are most associated with passing?
  2. How does the pass rate change from one group to another, for example between students who study more and less?
  3. Which combinations of circumstances have the lowest pass rates?
  4. Can a student’s grade in term 1 or term 2 warn us early that they may fail?
  5. Are the patterns the same in maths and Portuguese, in both schools, and for girls and boys?

Who it’s for

  • Teachers and class directors can learn the early warning signs of failing, such as a low term 1 grade, past failures or many absences, and look out for them in their own classes so support can start in time.
  • School counsellors and psychologists can see which circumstances often go with failing, to guide conversations with students and families.
  • School management can decide where support programmes and resources are likely to help most.
  • Students and parents can see which habits tend to go with passing.

Factors associated with passing the final grade

Each bar shows how much the pass rate differs between the best and the worst group of a factor, in percentage points (pts).

Pass rate by group

Each bar is one group of students. The blue part shows how many passed and the orange part how many failed.

Pass rate for two factors combined

Choose two factors. Each square shows the pass rate of the students who share both, with the number of students below it. The more orange the square, the fewer students passed.

Early grades and passing the final grade

Each point shows the share of students with that term grade who went on to pass the final grade. Switch between term 1 and term 2 with the buttons.

About the data

The data comes from a survey and the school records of 395 maths students and 649 Portuguese-language students at two secondary schools in Portugal, Gabriel Pereira and Mousinho da Silveira, in the 2005–2006 school year. It was collected by P. Cortez and A. Silva (2008) and is published in the UCI Machine Learning Repository as “Student Performance”.

Some students have a final grade of 0 even though their grades during the year were close to or above the pass mark. They most likely didn’t sit the final exam, so we hide them by default to stop them pulling the pass rates down.
