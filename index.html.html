What goes with a good grade?

By Folashade Ogunajo and Vanessa Ogoru, Information Visualisation 2026/2027, DETI, University of Aveiro

Final grades of secondary-school students at two schools in Portugal, next to their study habits, family and social life. Grades run from 0 to 20, and 10 is a pass. Start with the ranking, choose a factor to look closer, then follow groups of students across the charts.

Passed (10 or more) Failed (under 10) No final grade (did not sit the exam)

Which circumstances go with the biggest grade gaps?

Each row is one factor. Circles are the average final grade of each group within it (bigger circle, more students). The longer the line, the bigger the gap. Choose a row to look closer.

How are grades spread?

Every dot is one student, placed at their final grade. The small triangle marks each group’s median. Choose a group’s name to select those students in every chart; point at a dot to see that student.

Which combinations lead to failing?

Students flow from left to right through three factors to their final result. Band width is the number of students; colour is how they ended up. Choose a block or a band to select those students.

Do the first terms predict the final grade?

Each line is a path from term 1 to term 2 to the final grade; thicker lines are paths shared by more students. Drag along any axis to select students in that grade range.

About the data

Survey and school records of 395 maths students and 649 Portuguese-language students from Gabriel Pereira and Mousinho da Silveira secondary schools, school year 2005–2006. Collected by P. Cortez and A. Silva (2008) and published in the UCI Machine Learning Repository as “Student Performance”.

Students with a final grade of 0 almost all had passing or near-passing grades during the year, so they most likely did not sit the final exam. They are hidden by default because they would drag group averages down. The charts show associations, not causes: a factor that goes with lower grades does not by itself make grades lower.
